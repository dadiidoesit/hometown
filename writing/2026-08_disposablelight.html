A Disposable History of Light
08.26
a woman hanging on a fence

Hello there, the man said to the younger woman hanging from the fence. She lifted her head as much as she could do so in order to look down at him from where she was hanging. but that hurt her quite badly, and she stopped quickly.

Several moments passed with her eyes clutched tight, trying to push down the agonizing pain of her neck muscles contracting involuntarily from the stress being put upon them, on top of the gut-wrenching unwinding of her intestinal tract after it had sloughed out of her. When she could eventually perceive the outside world again, she said: Hello there yourself.

I haven't seen you hanging around here before. he said, but she didn't seem to think it was funny in the least bit to be made fun of.

She said: If you would kindly help-- And then She tried to gesture with one of her hanging hands towards a nice-sized leaded pipe not too far from them. It was rusted all around in big patches, and the paint had oxidized and begun flaking off in rough chunks. A perfectly sized pipe for bashing just lying halfway in the gutterspace between curb and street; the discard from some poor saps collection of would-be-useful-someday.

He didn't care to touch the pipe, though. and besides that he did not wish or want to do any violence at the moment. Later on he might reconsider it. Later on he had to meet with his wife and sister, and very soon was already going to be a few minutes now. If he got involved with this one, he'd certainly be late. Definitely would, judging by the sound of the too-distant screaming sirens.

The rest of you fell off. He noticed, gesturing with gingerly politeness at a pair of legs lying on the ground. Astonishingly they were are the very same pair that had sloughed off from her, he is informed, and he is astonished at the coincidence. The iron bars had been supportive in the divorce after unwinding a length of her internal tract who had lost the support of bone and epidermis when they took leave to the earth.

Does it hurt?

Terribly. It's one of the worst pains I've ever felt, but still some better than how I've felt. This interaction is steadily coming to rival the discomfort however.

He smiled to laugh at that, finding it genuinely funny, and he did say so to affirm the sentiment as real.

I find that quite a funny sentiment. I am sorry that your attempt to disembark went so awry. He thought he should behave more upset and disgusted to see a soontobe corpse, the remnants of something so human-shaped and still identifiably alike-like that it could be called alive for all intents and purposes of conversation and attack.

I thought for certainly I would lose consciousness when I came down. but instead I've only just lost my mind, I think. She replied, being more truthful to herself than considering his considerations. To the pain, more than anything. I might only be half here anymore. I really can't tell. That joke did not make him laugh, for she said it so earnestly it did not seem intentional and he would give her no credit thus. Her resentment was extraordinary, but all unspoken.

He asked her a question.

Yes, She replied with no hesitation whatsoever, proverbial heels dug into the durt under which she would never stand aga in upright. That bit of her liver which was skewered like kebab meat on the iron post seized and all the nerves that weren't dead cried in agony. Unlike life, un-life was a more immediately perpetual agony; this agony he was well acquainted with and from afar he had recognized her as being embroiled in it deeply as to nearly be his kinfolk and not just by the pool of blood and waste that gathered underneath the tragic scene of her mostly complete demise. Such observations of the human condition were like parlor tricks to him. he would never share in the awe or wonder solicited in those who were less intuitive and skilled in their own ability to observiate.

She continued: But you have no right, none at all, to speak to me as if I made this choice myself, sir. to end up so unencumbered of the weight below my spine? Had you considered not that I might have been pushed, or coerced into falling but not of my fully free and honestly willful intentions?

No. He said plainly to her without a moment of hesitation at all. He had been anticipating the question. the dreadfully dead and bloodlessly pale expression of her countenance had shifted into a screwed up glare of revulsion. He could smell the flies coming off of her intestines, but she was invisible to his senses just as she could not see anything light into him. and he wondered if she noticed that too, and might make some effort to invite less smelly guests.

In this half-dying state she became privy to the impermeable membrane of consciousness around which they were both wrapped, which we are all wrapped, and his thoughts became sensations upon her fleeting mind. His rudeness was rude and blunt and that had settled it: she would pray to the almighty with her last thoughts to fell this vile man, who most assuredly was kin to the devil.

At this point there was a scene gathering. It consisted of exactly one additional person who kept their measured distance while also trying to get a better view on what was happening. the understandably cautious decision to weigh the pros and cons of getting mixed up in the business of strangers and whatall. Another person joined in behind the first; that was an old woman of 77 years old whose burthday would be celebrated in just a week from today (leave it to you, reader, to decide which date was meant to prophesy her special day), now accompanied by one who had approached from the street.

How remarkable to find hanging on, or off of, a fence was a girl barely 19 years round the sun. As anyone could see, that could not be questioned, because as all know: the spine of a book tells as much as its contents, And truly she had bared all of her spine out to the world, hanging out of her with such grotesque exhibitionistic flair, and so all assumptions should be treated as equally valid.

Is she alright? One asked. Another opined aloud in similar fashion to no one in particular. A phone came out of someone's pocket and now the poor young woman's worst moment was immortalized. Little circuits housing arrays of 1s and 0s that would protect some fragment of her light as a prisoner and release it only when all power had sapped from the thing.

HE stepped to the side realizing he was blocking the scene from anyone with more authority than his own. She tried to follow his movements but her head, stuck in place as it was, could not properly rotate and then even that little bit of movement she was capable of was such excruciatingly painful that just a tiny shock of movement through tendon and muscle was enough to shut down any attempt to do anything other than hang in relative comfort. There were signs across the street that warned against this sort of thing; painted up in bright red and black so that it immediately drew powerful connotations of fear and power. And they said: nothing; but they read out loud the same wet sensation of being outside on dewy nights like these where the dark glowed with just a little prompting, more so in the moonlight because la luna promised us all sight beyond sight who open their eyes under her gaze. The signs said fear what is in the darkness, because it is powerful and if you open your eyes wide enough to see them they will see you and pursue. Perhaps the girl who was not yet a woman had seen something up on the roof of the hotel that she had checked into for the night. but this was a waning gibbous moon tonight, and she was looking after other affairs.

Could we help you up? He asked. The first kind thing he'd done all of today was asking this question. Ignorant but well intentioned in some moments, he was not unlike most others like him. He would make decisions that sometimes align with something approaching or arriving at goodness, and sometimes not.

The skin he wore tightened into a hopeful smile, but again she could not and would not turn her head to look at him. No, instead, She stared down at the ground and imagined his words written out in the bumps and discolorations. The light leaving her body made it hard to see the ground clearly but, if she focused hard enough and directed all her remaining energy into her eyes, she could see better than she'd ever seen before. Suddenly she could see more clearly than she'd ever seen.

Her brain had tried to make the infinite insanity of a noisy reality into something rational, like any brain might do. And there, refracting in the blood pooling into a long crimson stream following the steady pull of gravity, became a flood streaking over the modest dells made by the raised protrusions of the cement surface that glinted for every flash and flicker of electric lighting that was cast down upon it... Capturing and imprisoning some little partition to be forever hidden away, alike to how those wicked handheld computers had taken prisoner some portion of the not-girl's flicker; her soul. Somewhere in the interplay between the red river's shadow and how it caught the sterile pale blue LED lighting above, and the light from a few grains of sand all refracted inside the fluid membrane. What ways collided combined and began dancing together swirling like actual ribbons of light. Though it moved so quickly as to be instantaneous, the fading complex of neural light flashing in her skull was able to focus on breaking every single organic frame of information into a mental flip book of a sort.

She watched how the ribbons became a radiant fabric and seemed to bounce around with intention if not consciousness, because the precision and artistry and beauty of the paths they took were so exquisite that the woman-girl could not rationalize that it was not deliberate. If such things could be accidents would there be a point to living?

That question felt invigorating down to her core; it reminded her of warm porridge in the mornings turned yellow with too much butter, far too much butter - and that memory of warmth started settling in the stomach that hung underneath her. From there it radiated up into her chest, and up to the corner peaks of her shoulders, and up into her ears. She settled herself to determine if it could be an accident, and such accidents could be beautiful, and her brain obliged her in this decision. Settling into this new outlook she began the long task of obsessing over every detail of every organic frame of memory, until she had become a master of light and could speak its language as well as she could remember her own time as starlight. All of what had been her mind before was stretched out across the entire thin membrane of the universe in anticipation of that elastic moment when it would be snapped back into one single moment in all of what we call time.

To find out if it would take an eternity or just a moment, she committed herself to moving forward despite the detached nature of her usual ambulatory mechanisms.

What could be more human than to never give up the ghosts of self-reliance and aspiration?
