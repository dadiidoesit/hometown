Hello.

I've been running games for about ten years and playing them for twenty, and I started in play-by-post forums and IRC channels where a good story didn't need miniatures, expensive books or even a particularly good plot; sometimes all it needed was a few interesting people willing to take each other seriously for a couple of hours. I still think that's true.

I'm also a published writer, illustrator and voice actor, which turns out to be useful when six adventurers are trying to agree on how to get through a locked door. I make my own maps, tokens and handouts. The NPCs have voices. Some of them shouldn't.

What I'm selling is preparation you don't have to do, and a table that keeps meeting. If that sounds like something your group has been failing to organise for a year, it is.

WHAT A NIGHT LOOKS LIKE

Four to six players, an original story that answers what you actually do, and a handful of voices. Most games run about three hours. I use maps and tokens when they help clarify a fight and skip them when they'd turn a conversation into an accounting exercise.

There's a conversation before anyone pays: who's at the table, what kind of story you want, anything you'd rather not have come up, and when you can all realistically meet. That part is free and it isn't a game. It's the part where I find out what you actually want instead of guessing, which is also the part most games skip.
