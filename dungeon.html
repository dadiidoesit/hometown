Hello.

I've been running games for about ten years and playing them for twenty, and I started in play-by-post forums and IRC channels where a good story didn't need miniatures, expensive books or even a particularly good plot; sometimes all it needed was a few interesting people willing to take each other seriously for a couple of hours. I still think that's true.

I'm also a published writer, illustrator and voice actor, which turns out to be useful when six adventurers are trying to agree on how to get through a locked door. I make my own maps, tokens and handouts, the NPCs get voices (some of which they shouldn't have), and the villains get reasons that make sense from where they're standing.

What I'm selling is preparation you don't have to do and a table that keeps meeting, which is the thing most groups have spent a year failing to organise by the time they get to me.

WHAT A NIGHT LOOKS LIKE

Four to six players, an original story that answers what you actually do, and a handful of voices. Most games run about three hours. I use maps and tokens when they help clarify a fight and skip them when they'd turn a conversation into an accounting exercise.

There's a conversation before anyone pays: who's at the table, what kind of story you want, anything you'd rather not have come up, and when you can all realistically meet. That part is free and it isn't a game. It's the part where I find out what you actually want instead of guessing at it across six weeks of sessions.

WHAT YOU'RE ACTUALLY PAYING FOR

Most of what you're paying for is the boring half of it: turning up, having the maps drawn, remembering what happened last time, and keeping four people who have all had a week moving in the same direction. Over five years we moved this game's night around more times than I can count, because shift work and small children don't respect a calendar, and the campaign survived because the story and the people in it were worth coming back to; what never moved was me, which is the only part of a schedule you can actually hire.

I don't arrive cold either, and I don't read the module aloud. The NPCs have voices, the fights have weight, and the descriptions are written before you sit down rather than recited off a page; there's a real difference between a game where somebody performs it for you and one where somebody narrates their own notes, and you can hear which one you've got in the first ten minutes.

Then there's the part that isn't about the story at all. Everyone at the table having a good time is somebody's job, and at my table it's mine rather than yours; no bigotry, no creeping on other players, no talking over people, no picking on whoever happens to be the quietest. Anyone can stop a scene without explaining why, and if someone can't manage any of that then they don't play, because I'd rather run a table of four who behave than six who don't. It's also why somebody who has never played before can sit down, be terrible at it for an hour, and still want to come back next week.
